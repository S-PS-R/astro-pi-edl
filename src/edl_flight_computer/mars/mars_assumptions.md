# Mars simulation assumptions

This point-mass model is for the EDL classroom demo. Settings and thresholds live in `run.py`. The supplied landing case is tuned for a repeatable run.

- **Mars and atmosphere.** Mars is a rotating sphere with a radius of 3,389.5 km, a rotation period of 24.6229 hours, and gravity given by μ/r² (μ = 4.2828 × 10¹³ m³/s²). Entry and chute descent use a Mars-fixed frame with Coriolis and centrifugal acceleration. The atmosphere rotates with Mars and uses ideal CO₂ at 214 K, with surface pressure of 636 Pa and an exponential density profile.
- **Entry.** Starts at 120 km with inertial speed 3,000 m/s and flight-path angle −1°. Entry mass is 3,308.98 kg, reference area is 15 m², Cd is 1.5, Cl is 0.5, and the heating model uses a 4.5 m nose radius. A proportional bank command steers toward a waypoint 0.90° east of the landing site and is limited to ±10°. Heating uses Sutton–Graves heat flux. Attitude dynamics, heat shield temperature, and ablation are omitted.
- **Wind and speed.** Default wind uses seed 40, a horizontal surface wind of 2–10 m/s, and gusts up to 3 m/s that vary over 20-second intervals. Both fade with altitude using a 20 km scale height. Air-relative velocity drives Mach, heating, dynamic pressure, drag, and lift. Along-path acceleration uses ground-relative speed. Other wind seeds can change where the lander touches down.
- **Parachute and separation.** The 21.35 m disk-gap-band parachute opens while descending at or below Mach 1.8 and 800 Pa, at an altitude of at least 6 km. The canopy applies full drag immediately, with Cd taken from a Mach table. Vehicle and canopy drag are added as forces. Lift, canopy inflation dynamics, and payload swing are omitted after deployment. The heat shield (264.72 kg) drops 15 seconds later. The backshell and chute (189.9 kg together) separate while descending at or below 2 km and 100 m/s. Each separation changes the mass and drag area without a change in velocity.
- **Powered descent.** Engines ignite 0.5 seconds after backshell separation. From separation onward, the ground is a flat plane tangent to Mars at the separation point, gravity acts downward, and rotation forces are omitted. The lander uses steerable thrust up to 26 kN, 220 s specific impulse, and 360 kg of usable propellant. Fuel flow is thrust divided by specific impulse and standard Earth gravity. Vertical braking has priority. The controller stops diverting below 180 m and damps sideways drift.
- **Terminal descent and touchdown.** The terminal-speed event requires one simulated second within 5% of the local canopy equilibrium speed and within 0.10 m/s² of zero vertical acceleration. The powered lander switches to vertical descent below 0.1 m/s horizontal speed (inclusive) when close to the target or below the divert cutoff. The landing warning uses altitude divided by current descent rate during vertical descent. It is an estimate. A soft touchdown requires vertical descent and speeds at or below 2.5 m/s vertical and 0.2 m/s horizontal. A target hit also requires touchdown within 50 m of 49.02° N, 188.15° E. Contact ends the run.
- **Integration and outputs.** Forward Euler uses steps no larger than 0.02 simulated seconds. Event crossings are refined within a step. Downrange follows the entry great circle and crossrange is positive to its left. Telemetry longitudes may wrap to ±180°. Sense HAT readings are logged and checked for faults independently of the trajectory.
